# Rival colonies — decisions before implementation

Status: implemented after the user approved the simplified plan. The behavioral questionnaire is closed. Confirmed rules below remain the reference; historical "open" numeric/algorithm details are now covered by the implementation defaults here, rather than requiring more questions.

## Implemented defaults and verification

- Shared map/food with independent colony state and pheromone fields. Default two colonies, startup count 1–8, automatic/manual placement, live add/remove. Equal selected adult counts, 36 reserves, zero brood. Nest clearance 32, separation 100; no terrain edits for placement.
- Surplus pays 4 food for one brood every 8 seconds; maturation takes 40 seconds with no food and approaches 20 seconds with abundant stored food. Development rate is `1 + reserve / (reserve + 100)` and updates continuously for existing brood, with no progress reset or extra maturation charge. Existing brood matures without food or adults. Adult restoration restarts the normal starvation grace. Total adults plus brood capped at 350.
- At encounters within 7 units and unobstructed contact, ants engage for at least one simulated second before equal odds select a loser; its colony's retreat chance (initially 30% for all) selects retreat or death. Seeded shuffled processing, one pending engagement per pair, multiple local opponents allowed. Fighting ants stay in place and emit alarms; removal or death cancels invalid engagements. No explicit numerical bonus, cooldown, health system, or combat energy changes.
- One cargo per ant. Stored reserves are stolen first, including fractional remainder; only then does one brood become one cargo food unit. Cargo transfers directly to the winning opponent. Adults yield no food. No mutual-death branch is generated by the quick resolver; the agreed cargo-loss rule still applies if one is added later.
- Shared recruitment fraction defaults 25%. Stable distributed willingness per ant selects a subset, avoiding repeated random rolls recruiting everyone. Own alarms within local perception guide empty-handed responders; carriers excluded. No retreat immunity or recruitment cooldown. Finding no enemy releases a responder; stale signals without enemies cannot immediately recruit it again.
- Alarm half-life 3 seconds, or half the food-trail half-life if smaller. Emitted on fight/flee only. Alarm is an internal field; current canvas shows colony food trails without adding a separate alarm overlay.
- 49 automated tests and production build pass. Safari checks verified the running layout, pause, automatic live addition, full removal, manual live placement, and keyboard food placement. Longer balance/performance playtesting remains useful.

All numbers and low-level choices in this section are implementation defaults, not additional user decisions.

## Confirmed direction

- Several distinct colonies compete for food and territory.
- Rival nests also act as food sources through their food stores and brood, providing an incentive to raid them. Brood is slowly produced and eventually matures into adult ants; quantities, costs, and timing remain undecided.
- Answer 1, corrected: adult ants are never food. Killing an adult creates no edible biomass; only food it was carrying can transfer under the agreed encounter rules. A rival nest's food stores and brood are both food sources. This supersedes the earlier ambiguous "ants/brood" interpretation.
- Initial brood: colonies start with adults and food reserves only, with zero brood; brood is produced during the run.
- Brood lifecycle confirmed: food supports gradual brood production, and brood matures into adults after a delay. Population replacement must be slow; do not retain direct food-to-adult spawning in the planned rival version. Production and maturation rates/costs are implementation tuning; use conservative shared defaults.
- Maturation clarification: existing brood continues developing when stored food runs out. The user's briefly proposed pause rule was withdrawn; do not pause maturation or add brood starvation deaths. This does not authorize producing new brood without food.
- Extinction clarification: a colony becomes extinct only when both its adult count and brood count are zero. Zero adults with surviving brood is not extinction; brood can still mature and restore adults. Once both are gone, extinction remains permanent.
- Nest-loot priority: take stored food first; brood is available to raiders only when the rival nest's stored food is empty.
- Answer 2: raiders can pick up stored food immediately on reaching a rival nest. There is no raid timer or requirement to defeat nearby defenders before pickup; fighting is handled by separate inter-ant interactions.
- Rival encounters usually lead to fighting or fleeing, except that two food-carrying rivals do not fight. Fleeing ants surrender carried food directly to the rival; no food is left on the ground. A fight ends when an ant dies or retreats. Exact engagement, probability, and retreat rules remain open.
- Answer 3: nests cannot be damaged or destroyed directly. Raids steal food/brood and kill adults through inter-ant encounters. Extinction requires zero adults and zero brood.
- Answer 5, updated: fights last at least one simulated second before a probabilistic outcome: one ant dies or an ant retreats. Do not introduce a prolonged health/damage exchange; outcome probabilities and modifiers remain undecided.
- Answer 6: multiple ants can gang up on one rival; fights are not restricted to isolated pairs. Numerical advantage is a desired future refinement, but the user declined even a lightweight headcount bias for the first version. Start with equal individual combat odds without headcount modifiers. Retreat probability remains independent of local numbers.
- Answer 7: a killed ant's carried food transfers directly to an attacker. If both ants die, the food is lost. Encounters must not create food piles or clutter on the ground. Which attacker receives it in group combat, and carrier capacity, remain open.
- Follow-up confirmed: food surrendered while fleeing transfers directly to the rival. When both rival ants carry food, they pass each other without fighting or transferring food; each keeps its cargo.
- Answer 8: use one ant type for now. Existing workers and scouts can fight; do not introduce a separate soldier caste. Scouting remains a behavioral assignment, not a separate fighting type.
- Answer 9: an empty-handed rival can engage a food carrier under the ordinary encounter rules; the winner takes or retains the food. There is no separate always-surrender policy for carriers. The exception remains that two carriers pass peacefully with their own cargo. Fleeing still transfers cargo to the rival.
- Retreat destination: move away from the encounter and resume foraging, rather than returning to the nest. No cooldown follows retreat: ants remain eligible for encounters and alarm recruitment immediately. Disengagement movement details remain open.
- Answer 10: retreat is decided by a random chance configured per colony. All colonies use the same retreat chance for now; nearby allies/enemies do not modify that chance. The numerical probability and timing of the retreat roll remain unset. This does not yet define how group size affects the chance of winning or dying.
- Answer 11: combat has no additional energy effects. Fighting adds no energy cost, and remaining energy does not modify fight/retreat probabilities. Existing movement, fatigue, and recovery remain controlled by the energy toggle.
- Answer 12: include an enemy-detected pheromone emitted when an ant fights or flees. Merely seeing a rival does not emit it. The peaceful two-carrier pass therefore does not emit this signal.
- Alarm decay: enemy-detected pheromone fades faster than food pheromone, so old fights stop drawing ants relatively quickly. The exact rate remains tuning, not a confirmed number.
- Answer 13: ants respond only to pheromones from their own colony, including food and enemy-detected signals. Rival pheromone fields do not attract, repel, or guide them. Direct encounters with rival ants still follow the established interaction rules.
- Answer 14: recruit an adjustable fraction of nearby empty-handed ants to enemy-detected pheromone. Food carriers are ineligible. The recruitment fraction is one shared setting across all colonies. The starting fraction, selection method, and proximity range remain open.
- Answer 15: once recruited, an ant prioritizes the enemy signal over nearby food. Encountering food does not make it abandon recruitment. Response-ending rules are recorded under answer 16.
- Answer 16: a recruited ant ends its response when its fight resolves, by winning or retreating, and resumes ordinary behavior under the existing food/cargo rules. If it reaches the alarm area and finds no enemies, it immediately resumes foraging instead of waiting for the signal to fade. Retreat adds no recruitment cooldown; selection and stale-alarm handling remain open.
- Answer 17: territory emerges from colony activity and pheromone trails, with no explicit ownership or capture mechanic. No separate territory field or overlay has been selected; own-colony-only scent recognition is unchanged.
- Answer 18: configure colony count at startup and allow adding/removing colonies during a run without resetting. Default count, allowed range, and live addition details remain open. Manual removal deletes all colony-owned ants, nest, food reserves, brood, and pheromone fields; nothing remains as loot.
- Answer 19: offer both seeded automatic and manual nest placement. Every colony starts with equal adult populations and food reserves and zero brood. Exact shared starting values, placement restrictions, and live placement details remain open.
- Answer 20: the player is an observer placing shared food, not issuing commands to ants or a selected colony. Preserve the agreed setup controls and minimal simulation controls.
- Manual removal is separate from natural extinction: everything belonging to the removed colony disappears, including food and alarm pheromones. Natural extinction still leaves a lootable nest.
- Answer 21: an extinct colony's nest remains on the map, and its leftover stored food remains available for other colonies to loot. Looting does not revive the extinct colony.
- Answer 22: continue the sandbox when only one colony survives. Do not automatically stop the run or declare a winning end state; extinction remains permanent and does not trigger replacement colonies.
- Each colony's nest, ants, and trails share a color family.
- Keep the simulation and interface minimal, and let local behavior and feedback produce outcomes rather than prescribing optimal ants.
- Preserve these confirmed behaviors. Choose routine implementation defaults without more design questions and label them as tuning rather than user decisions. Record useful findings here, in PROJECT_NOTES.md, and in AGENTS.md.

## Enemy-detected pheromone

Confirmed trigger: an ant fighting or fleeing emits enemy-detected pheromone. Mere sightings and peaceful carrier crossings do not trigger it. Only its own colony responds, through an adjustable fraction of nearby empty-handed ants; food carriers are ineligible. Recruited ants prioritize the enemy signal over food, end their response after winning/retreating, and immediately resume foraging if no enemies remain on arrival. There is no retreat cooldown or temporary recruitment exclusion. Enemy pheromones fade faster than food trails. Use simple implementation defaults for selection, exact decay, and stale-alarm handling; these do not require further interview questions.

## Simplified build plan

The user has supplied enough behavioral direction. Stop asking about selection algorithms, radii, probabilities, capacity edge cases, and individual timers. Keep these as ordinary implementation choices and tune through observation. The user subsequently approved this plan, and the four steps are implemented.

1. **Multiple colonies on the existing map.** Give each colony its own nest, adults, reserves, brood, and food/alarm pheromone fields with matching colors. Reuse existing foraging, terrain, and energy behavior. Support startup count and automatic/manual placement, plus live add/remove. Manual removal clears the entire colony; natural extinction leaves its nest lootable.
2. **Simple lifecycle and raiding.** Start with equal adults/reserves and zero brood. Surplus food slowly produces brood, which matures after a delay even if food runs out. Raiders collect stored food first, then brood, using the existing carrying/return behavior. Only zero adults and zero brood means permanent extinction.
3. **Quick encounters.** Use a shared initial retreat probability and equal individual win odds; transfer existing cargo directly to the rival. Two carriers pass peacefully. Retreat changes direction and resumes foraging with no cooldown. Allow multiple attackers through local encounters, without coordinated group mechanics, numerical bonuses, health bars, or combat energy effects.
4. **Simple alarm response and verification.** Fighting/fleeing deposits a short-lived own-colony alarm. The shared recruitment fraction selects some nearby empty-handed ants; responders prioritize the alarm and resume normal behavior after resolution or finding no enemy. Preserve minimal controls and show adult/brood/reserve counts per colony. Verify cargo conservation, signal isolation, slow growth, extinction/removal, connected placement, and seeded repeatability; run the existing tests and build.

### Keep parameters basic

- Reuse existing initial adults/reserves, food carrying, upkeep, and energy tuning where practical.
- Keep a small shared configuration for retreat chance, recruitment fraction, brood production cost/interval, and maturation delay. Recruitment fraction is the requested user-facing adjustment; other values can remain code defaults initially.
- Use existing perception/pheromone machinery where practical, with alarm decay faster than food-trail decay. Avoid exposing separate radius, selection, cooldown, capacity, and group-combat controls.
- Document chosen values and observed problems during implementation. Do not label untested tuning as scientifically validated or as a user-selected rule.
