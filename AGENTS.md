## User design decisions

- Aim for a relaxing sandbox.
- Keep the UI basic.
- Keep the interface bare bones: simulation canvas, compact controls, and useful stats. No header, hero/title section, branding, taglines, decorative cards, custom typography, or ornamental styling. Prefer native controls and plain functional labels. The user explicitly prioritizes minimalism and rejected the initial styled presentation.
- Represent ants as dots for now; do not implement detailed ant graphics.
- Show live adult population beside every colony nest as well as in per-colony stats. Provide live control of food quantity per placement and simulation speeds beyond 4× while keeping the interface minimal.
- Keep long-range exploration to a small scout minority. Unproductive trips should cause workers to return and reduce preference for repeatedly unsuccessful directions.
- Render pheromone trails smoothly; avoid visible grid cells or blocky heatmaps.
- Next planned milestone: rival colonies. A colony's nest, ants, and trails must share a coherent color family. Keep colony identity colors separate from shared food/terrain colors; indicate carried food with a small food-colored dot rather than recoloring the ant into another identity.
- Maintain trail contrast across medium/high strengths and between nearby paths. Overlaps should progressively darken and may eventually blend, without saturating or blurring together prematurely. Preserve the bare-bones interface when adjusting colors.
- Generate procedural obstacle maps with no unreachable open pockets. All walkable terrain must connect to the nest; preserve physical clearance and avoid diagonal corner shortcuts.
- Let competing food routes emerge and improve over time. Ants must not receive a precomputed shortest return path or a direct path-length reward. Prefer subtle physical costs and pheromone feedback to prescribing the winning route.
- Energy is a subtle experimental pressure, not a guarantee of optimality. Preserve exploration and compare energy on/off across seeds before claiming efficiency improvements.
- Use TypeScript and Vite. The current implementation uses Canvas 2D.
- The initial prototype runs one colony. The current version supports several rival colonies after the completed behavior discussion.
- Allow the user to click to place food in the first version.
- Food does not replenish automatically; ants depend on the user adding more.
- Running out of food must affect colony population.
- More food can grow the colony, including restoring population lost to shortages, while the colony is still alive.
- A colony can die out completely. Extinction is permanent for that colony: food cannot revive it, and it must never automatically respawn.
- Record design answers in this file as well as the project notes.

## Current implementation

- Rivalry is implemented. `simulation/sandbox.ts` coordinates a shared terrain/food supply and independent `World` colony engines. The UI uses Sandbox; World remains usable for isolated tests/experiments.
- Starts with two colonies; startup count 1–8, both automatic/manual placement, and live add/remove are supported. New colonies use the same selected starting population and 36 reserves, zero brood. Nests need 32 units of open clearance and 100 units separation; terrain stays immutable. Unique active color slots are reused after removal.
- Shared defaults in `RIVALRY`: retreat chance 0.3, recruitment fraction 0.25, brood cost 4, production interval 8 seconds of surplus, baseline maturation 40 developmental seconds, food scale 100 (rate `1 + reserve / (reserve + 100)`, approaching 2×), alarm half-life 3 seconds (clamped below the food half-life), encounter radius 7, fight duration 1 simulated second. These are implementation tuning, not user-selected numbers.
- Each eligible encounter picks a loser with equal odds, then uses that colony's retreat chance to choose retreat versus death. A pair has a pending one-second engagement before resolution; participants stay in place, continue normal upkeep, and emit alarms while fighting. Removal/death clears invalid engagements; multiple opponents can engage, with no headcount bonus. Order is seeded/shuffled. No retreat immunity or combat energy cost. Cargo transfers to the immediate winning opponent; no adult biomass or ground piles. A fractional last reserve unit stays fractional cargo; one stolen brood becomes one food unit.
- Alarm responders use a stable distributed willingness value per ant, compared with the shared fraction; no repeated random rolls that eventually recruit all ants. Nearby own-colony alarms guide eligible empty-handed ants, and stale alarms with no enemies do not keep them responding. Alarm fields are separate from food trails; the canvas currently displays food trails only.
- Brood entries store remaining baseline developmental seconds, not wall-clock deadlines. Each tick consumes `STEP * maturationRate`, preserving progress as food stores rise/fall. Maturation adds no extra food charge; production still costs 4 food every 8 seconds of surplus.
- Extinction requires zero adults and zero brood. Brood maturation continues without food/adults; the ordinary starvation grace restarts when brood restores an adult-less colony. Natural extinct nests remain lootable; manual removal clears the whole colony and its fields. New simulation resets the world; explicit live addition creates a new colony identity.

- The user authorized implementation after answering the design questions. The Vite + TypeScript application is in `web/`, with Canvas 2D rendering and an independent simulation engine.
- Delivered food enters a shared reserve. Workers consume it over simulation time; surplus funds brood and sustained depletion reduces population after a grace period.
- Current tuning defaults: 48 initial workers, 36 reserve units, 80 food units per click by default (1–10,000 selectable), consumption of 0.006 units per worker per simulated second, 15-second starvation grace, loss of 3% of workers rounded up every 1.5 seconds thereafter, brood costing 4 units no faster than once every 8 seconds, produced when reserves exceed `population * 0.9 + 8`, with maturation taking 40 seconds unfed and approaching 20 seconds with abundant reserves, and a cap of 350 workers. These are implementation choices, not additional user requirements.
- Treat extinction as terminal only when both adult population and brood reach zero. A reset, if provided, starts a new colony rather than reviving the extinct one.
- Run `npm test` and `npm run build` from `web/` for simulation changes. Preserve seeded repeatability with identical timed user inputs.
- Speed options are 0.5×, 1×, 2×, 4×, 8×, 16×, and 32×. Keep fixed 30 Hz simulation ticks; bound per-frame simulation work (64 steps/12 ms) and accumulated backlog, and clear backlog when changing speed. Requested speed is best effort on slower devices.
- The simulation pauses progression when the tab is hidden; there is no offline progression or persistence yet.
- Foraging defaults: one scout per eight spawned ants; workers start with a 14–20-second search budget and may earn one 10-second extension when following a real trail beyond the nest clearing. Scouts search for 45–65 seconds. Worker departures favor nearby pheromone signals and penalize unsuccessful departure directions; failure memory has a 40-second half-life. Empty returns deposit no food pheromone. These are tunable implementation choices.
- Pheromones use continuous interpolation for sensing/deposition. Display-only blur and interpolated opacity soften the trail raster without changing the underlying evaporation rules.
- `rendering/palette.ts` separates world colors from a `ColonyPalette`; the renderer and trail raster accept a colony palette, with teal as the current default. The map uses earth-brown ground and darker brown obstacles; olive marks food. Keep the controls minimal. Active colonies have distinct palettes.
- Each colony has its own trail raster, ImageData, and canvas source. Do not reuse a mutable canvas across queued colony draws: earlier trail layers can be replaced by later ones. Recreate layers when a reset replaces their World instance and remove deleted-colony layers.
- Trail display uses a narrower `[1, 6, 1] / 8` separable blur, a light-to-deep colony-color ramp over density 0–12, and opacity `245 * density / (density + 4)`. This keeps higher strengths distinct and preserves a visible valley between nearby paths. These changes are display-only; field limits and ant behavior are unchanged.
- Maps are generated deterministically from the selected seed, using a separate random stream. Enclosed cavities become solid terrain; the nest has a protected clearing. `New map` chooses a fresh seed and starts a new colony, while `New simulation` reuses the selected seed.
- Exploration avoids walls and cannot sense food through them. All returners retrace their own recorded outbound route. Route memory erases revisited loops and straightens only visible bends within 24 units; it cannot choose an undiscovered obstacle bypass. `Terrain.homeWaypoint` remains a diagnostic utility and must not steer simulated ants. Food placement requires clearance, and pheromone deposits skip blocked cells.
- Loaded ants deposit 0.6 pheromone per actual unit walked, independent of route length, energy level, or return time. The previous direct route-quality multiplier and return-time decay were removed for the energy experiment. Worker trail attraction remains probabilistic, and visual opacity preserves medium/high strength differences.
- Energy defaults: 100 maximum, movement cost 0.012 per unit (15% more while carrying), recovery of 8 energy per simulated second at the nest, and up to 10% speed reduction at zero energy. Energy never immobilizes or directly kills an ant; normal colony consumption/starvation rules remain in force. Rest has no extra food charge and does not require reserves, avoiding a recovery/foraging deadlock.
- `World.energyEnabled` defaults true; the main control row's Energy checkbox supports on/off comparison and also applies to new colonies. Average energy is displayed. `npm run experiment:energy` runs eight paired seeded fork-map trials; distance, deliveries, and rest metrics are observational, not used by ants to rank routes. Loop erasure never refunds energy already spent.
- Known food-selection caveat: targeting currently uses the first visible food entry within 65 units, and pickup the first eligible entry within 14 units (`Array.find`). This creates insertion-order bias when multiple patches qualify. Do not describe this as nearest-food selection or an inherent ACO requirement. Nearest-visible selection is a proposed refinement, not yet implemented or approved as a design decision.
- Terrain is immutable during a run. Any future terrain editing must rebuild connectivity/navigation and invalidate the renderer's cached terrain.
